# NORTHWEST block — research received 2026-10-02 (WA, OR, NV, ID, MT, WY, UT, CO, NM, AK, HI)
Framing: legal information, NOT legal advice.

## WA
- Min age tattoo: 18 — flat ban, no parental-consent exception. RCW 26.28.085 makes tattooing any person under 18 a misdemeanor; only defense is bona fide attempt to ascertain age (e.g., requesting driver's license or other picture ID).
- Required disclosures: none found at state level — no state-mandated risk/permanence/aftercare language on the consent form.
- Retention: 2 years. WAC 308-22-070 requires client records: customer name, age, address; procedure date; practitioner name; location and type of procedure; customer signature.
- ID: no requirement to record or retain ID; ID enters only via the RCW 26.28.085 defense.
- Artist/license info: practitioner name in the client record (WAC 308-22-070). No license-number or shop-address requirement found.
- Barber/cosmetology: patch/strand-test concepts appear only in WAC 308-20-080 training curriculum. No state-required chemical-service consent form, patch-test rule, or minor-consent rule for chemical services found.
- Citations: RCW 26.28.085; WAC 308-22-070; WAC 308-20-080.

## OR
- Min age tattoo: 18 — flat ban. ORS 690.360(1)(a) prohibits tattooing anyone under 18 regardless of parental consent. Sole exception: physician-prescribed medical repigmentation.
- Required disclosures: OAR 331-915-0085 (EBAP rules, eff. 6/17/2025) — client record must contain client's signature acknowledging the following given verbally AND in writing: (a) explanation of the procedure, (b) risks, (c) potential complications/side effects, (d) adverse outcomes, (e) restrictions, (f) aftercare instructions. Record must also document medical/skin conditions (diabetes, cold sores/fever blisters, psoriasis/eczema, pregnancy/breastfeeding), sensitivities, bleeding history, and any complications.
- Retention: 3 years. First 90 days kept on premises; older records produced within 7 days on request.
- ID: yes — a copy of the client's government-issued photo ID must be obtained and included in the client record as proof of age.
- Artist/license info: licensee name + license number required in the client record, plus date and body location of the procedure. (Oregon is the first state found requiring license number in the record.)
- Barber/cosmetology: Oregon COS rules — chemical peels and dermaplaning require documented record of body location, medical/skin conditions, sensitivities, complications, referrals, chemical used (brand/manufacturer, type, layers, time on skin, pre/post care); initial-visit signature that client received, understood, and consented to the procedure.
- Citations: ORS 690.360; OAR 331-915-0085; Oregon Board of Cosmetology rules (COS-Rules.pdf).

## NV
- Min age tattoo: 18 at county level. Tattooing is regulated at county level — no state statute and no state-level form requirements. (Central Nevada Health District rules as exemplar; other counties may differ.)
- Parental consent: yes, under 18. CNHD §030.020: no invasive body decoration on a person under 18 without consent and proper ID of a parent, custodial parent, or guardian, or under physician's supervision. Emancipated minors treated as adults.
- Required disclosures: county level. CNHD §030.010: release form required before any invasive body decoration procedure. CNHD §030.025: written aftercare to each client must include: establishment name, address, phone number + artist's name; direction on when to consult a physician, including signs of infection and allergic reaction; operator may ask about client's history of blood-borne diseases (§030.009). Release form must state the procedure should be considered permanent.
- Retention: 2 years minimum. CNHD §030.015: release form (retained ≥2 years) must contain: artist's full name; procedure description; statement procedure should be considered permanent; client's full legal name, DOB, phone, address; copy of client's photo ID (and for minors, copy of parent/guardian's photo ID); date; signature(s).
- ID: copy of client's photo ID in the record; for minors, copy of parent/guardian's photo ID. (§030.015; §030.020 requires parent's "proper ID.")
- Artist/license info: artist's full name on release form (§030.010/§030.015) and on written aftercare (§030.025); establishment name/address/phone on aftercare.
- Barber/cosmetology: NRS/NAC 644A — no chemical-service consent requirement found.
- Citations: Central Nevada Health District Body Art Regulations §§030.009, 030.010, 030.015, 030.020, 030.025. Proposed (2016) LCB draft R112-16 would require express in-person custodial-parent consent for minors — adoption status UNVERIFIED.

## ID
- Min age tattoo: 14 with consent; 18 without. Idaho Code §18-1523: no tattoo on anyone under 14, period; ages 14–17 require prior written informed consent of a parent/guardian.
- Parental consent: yes, ages 14–17 only. Must be prior, written, informed consent of parent/guardian, executed in the presence of the person performing the procedure (or their employee/agent). No notary requirement. Statute does not specify whether parent must remain present during the procedure.
- Required disclosures: none at state level. Retention: none at state level. ID: none at state level.
- Artist/license info: none at state level — Idaho has no state tattoo licensing or sanitation oversight.
- Barber/cosmetology: no state-level chemical-service consent requirement found (IDAPA 24.28.01 covers licensing, sanitation, schools; no consent-form mandate located).
- Citations: Idaho Code §18-1523. County/city-level rules may exist — UNVERIFIED.

## MT
- Min age tattoo: under the age of majority only with explicit in-person parental consent; 18+ unrestricted. MCA §45-5-623(1)(e) makes tattooing a child under the age of majority without the parent's/guardian's explicit in-person consent a misdemeanor. DPHHS guidance: a fake ID is insufficient to prove parenthood.
- Parental consent: yes. ARM 37.112.144: parent/legal guardian of client under 18 must sign the consent form in person before each procedure. ARM 37.112.158: parent must accompany the minor throughout the procedure.
- Required disclosures: ARM 37.112.144 mandates the following on the consent form, signed by client before each procedure: (a) name and address, date, design, body location, other info; (b) complications list — abscesses, allergies, excessive bleeding, heavy metal poisoning, infection, keloid, muscle/nerve paralysis, scarring, swelling, blood-borne pathogens, plus tongue swelling, throat closure, tooth fracture for oral procedures; (c) symptoms of infection; (d) instruction to consult a licensed medical provider; (e) statement that the procedure is permanent; (f) statement that client received aftercare instructions in writing and verbally and consents to the procedure.
- Retention: 3 years — per a Lake County, MT health-department plan review (secondary source); official ARM text UNVERIFIED.
- ID: no state-level photo-ID retention requirement found. DPHHS Q&A cautions fake ID does not establish the parent relationship.
- Artist/license info: ARM 37.112.142 — client record must include a copy of the signed consent form, the artist's name, the establishment's name/address/phone, the client's medical/skin information, and any physician referral (per 37.112.158).
- Barber/cosmetology: ARM 24.121 — no chemical-service consent requirement found (straight-razor facial shaving is within scope; SDS sheets required on premises).
- Citations: MCA §45-5-623; ARM 37.112.142; ARM 37.112.144; ARM 37.112.158; ARM 24.121.

## WY
- Min age tattoo: 18 (age of majority) without consent. Wyo. Stat. §14-3-107(a): no body art on a person under the age of majority except with parent/guardian consent.
- Parental consent: yes — parent/guardian's consent must be present at the time the procedure is performed. Whether consent must be in writing is UNVERIFIED (statute says "consent ... present at the time"; no form specified).
- Required disclosures: none found statewide. No statewide body-art sanitation or consent-form rules found — county-level regulation possible but UNVERIFIED.
- Retention: none found statewide.
- ID: artist must demand proof of age; valid forms: driver's license, Selective Service registration card, military ID, US passport, Eastern Shoshone/Northern Arapaho tribal ID, DOT-issued ID. Reasonable reliance on such ID is a defense. Violation is a misdemeanor punishable by up to 6 months and/or $750. No requirement to retain a copy found.
- Artist/license info: none found statewide.
- Barber/cosmetology: no state-level chemical-service consent requirement found (Board of Cosmetology rules address curricula and infection control).
- Citations: Wyo. Stat. §14-3-107.

## UT
- Min age tattoo: 18 without consent. Utah Code §76-10-2201 (renumbered effective 5/7/2025 by 2025 Ch. 173; definitions now at §76-9-2001) — tattooing a minor (<18, unmarried, not emancipated) without qualifying parental consent is an offense. No absolute age floor when consent conditions are met (2015's HB143 under-14 ban was a bill only, not enacted).
- Parental consent: yes, with three simultaneous requirements: (1) parent/guardian is present during the procedure; (2) proof of identity and familial relationship is provided; (3) written signed permission is given. No notary requirement found.
- Required disclosures: none at state level — county-level. Example: Tooele County HD Reg. #10 §5.1 requires verbal + written department-approved educational information; written aftercare advising client to consult a physician at first sign of abnormal infection or swelling, bearing establishment's name, address, phone; signed and dated by both parties (operator keeps original, client gets copy); plus prominently displayed department Disclosure Statement advising of risks and possible consequences.
- Retention: none mandated at state level.
- ID: defense provision — no actual knowledge of client's age plus review, photocopy, and retention of an apparently valid government-issued picture ID purporting the person is 18+ is a defense (class B misdemeanor otherwise, per HB0227). For minors, the present parent must show proof of identity/familial relationship.
- Artist/license info: none found statewide.
- Barber/cosmetology: R156-11a — before applying a chemical exfoliant, using a microneedling device, or using a microdermabrasion machine, the licensee must inform the client (a) the procedure may only be performed for cosmetic, not medical, purposes unless under supervision of a licensed health care practitioner, and (b) the benefits and risks of the procedure. Written format not specified in the text found.
- Citations: Utah Code §76-10-2201 (see also §76-9-2001 per 2025 Ch. 173); Utah Admin. Code R156-11a; Tooele County HD Reg. #10 §5.1 (county-level example).

## CO
- Min age tattoo: no age/minor provision in the state rule. 6 CCR 1010-22 (CDPHE body-art rule) contains no parental-consent or minimum-age provision — confirmed by text search of the rule; minor tattoo consent is handled at county level (e.g., Fremont County 2009 rules require signed parental consent for under 18; 2020 Boulder County proposal would add written consent + copy of parent's/guardian's state or federal photo ID — proposal, adoption UNVERIFIED).
- Required disclosures: 6 CCR 1010-22 §4-403 — consent form for all procedures must document that written AND verbal instructions regarding risks, outcome, and aftercare were given, including: (1) establishment's name, address, phone number and the name of the body artist who performed the procedure; (2) direction on when to consult a physician, including signs of infection, allergic reaction, expected duration of healing; (3) detailed description of how to care for the body-art site; (4) explanation that body art should be considered permanent; (5) possible side effects from the procedure. §4-402: client must be asked to disclose diabetes, hemophilia, skin diseases or lesions, allergies/adverse reactions to latex, pigments, dyes, disinfectants, soaps, or metals, treatment with anticoagulants or blood-thinners, and any other info aiding healing evaluation.
- Retention: 3 years minimum — client records maintained on the premises (§4-401).
- ID: none in the state rule.
- Artist/license info: §4-403 requires the establishment's name/address/phone and the name of the body artist who performed the procedure on the consent documentation.
- Barber/cosmetology: 4 CCR 731-1, Rule 1.9(D) — a predisposition patch test must be administered 24 hours prior to a chemical and/or manual resurfacing exfoliating procedure when the client is under a physician's supervision for skin disorders, is pregnant, or has had a medium/deep chemical peel, laser treatment, IPL, or plastic/reconstructive surgery within the previous 6 weeks (adverse reaction prohibits the product/procedure); written physician approval is the alternative.
- Citations: 6 CCR 1010-22 §§4-401, 4-402, 4-403; 4 CCR 731-1 Rule 1.9(D).

## NM
- Min age tattoo: none specified in the rule text. Minors require parental presence and written consent (see below); no age floor found — absence of a floor treated as UNVERIFIED beyond rule text reviewed.
- Parental consent: yes. 16.36.5.11(C): for a minor client, the record must include the signature of the client plus written proof of the parent's/legal guardian's presence and consent — both presence and written consent required. No notary requirement found.
- Required disclosures: 16.36.5.11(A)(1)-(2) — practitioner must inform the client verbally AND in writing that certain health conditions may increase risks — diabetes, hemophilia, skin disease/lesions/sensitivities, allergies to pigments or dyes, epilepsy/seizures/fainting/narcolepsy, anticoagulant use, hepatitis/HIV. Client signs a form confirming the information was provided, that they have no disqualifying condition, that they consent, and that they received aftercare instructions. §16.36.5.11(F): written aftercare instructions must cover proper cleansing; consulting a provider for redness/tenderness/swelling, rash, drainage, or fever within 24 hours; the establishment's address and phone number; a copy goes to the client (board model instructions referenced).
- Retention: 3 years; records must be destroyed by shredding (§16.36.5.11(E)).
- ID: no government-photo-ID requirement found in the rule text viewed.
- Artist/license info: record must include the licensee's name (§16.36.5.11(C)), plus type/location of procedure, ink colors with manufacturer/catalog/invoice info, and (for minors) written proof of parental presence/consent.
- Barber/cosmetology: 16.34.7 NMAC prohibits razor-edged devices (credo blades, callus shavers, rasps, graters) for skin/callus removal and prohibits MMA, fumigants, formalin, roll-on wax, and UV light boxes. No chemical-service consent requirement found.
- Citations: NMSA §61-17B-1 et seq. (Body Art Safe Practices Act); 16.36.5.11 NMAC; 16.34.7 NMAC.

## AK
- Min age tattoo: 18 — flat ban. AS 08.13.217(a): no person may tattoo or perform permanent cosmetic coloring on a minor. A criminally-negligent violation is a class B misdemeanor. (2000 SLA Ch. 93; amended by SLA Ch. 23.)
- Parental consent: not allowed for tattoos — ban is absolute. Parental consent allowed only for body piercing: AS 08.13.217(b) permits piercing a minor with the parent's/guardian's prior written permission plus the parent present during the procedure.
- Required disclosures: AS 08.13.215(a)-(b) — before the procedure, practitioner must give client written board-approved educational information; after the procedure, written board-approved aftercare instructions containing: (1) direction to consult a physician at the first sign of infection; (2) shop's name, address, phone number; (3) signed and dated by both the client and the practitioner — practitioner keeps original, client gets copy. §215(c): shop must prominently display (1) the board's health-risks statement (developed under AS 08.13.030(b)(3)) and (2) the names, addresses, phone numbers of the departments plus a description of how to file a complaint.
- Retention: body-piercing written parental permissions kept on file ≥3 years (§217(b)). Tattoo aftercare originals kept by practitioner, but no retention duration stated in statute — gap flagged.
- ID: none found.
- Artist/license info: shop name/address/phone on aftercare form (§215(b)); board's health-risks statement and department complaint info displayed (§215(c)).
- Barber/cosmetology: none found — same board (AS 08.13) regulates barbers/hairdressers/estheticians, but no chemical-service consent or patch-test requirement located.
- Citations: AS 08.13.217; AS 08.13.215; AS 08.13.030(b)(3).

## HI
- Min age tattoo: 18 without consent. HRS §321-379(b)(6): tattooing anyone under 18 without written consent of a parent/guardian is a violation (up to $10,000 per offense); consent forms must be kept at the shop ≥2 years in a confidential manner.
- Parental consent: yes — written consent of parent/guardian required. HAR §11-17-7(b) (eff. 9/18/1981): unlawful to tattoo a person under 18 without written parental/guardian consent; all written consents kept on file. No notary or in-person requirement found. (Under-16 flat ban from 2009's SB1263 was a bill draft only — never enacted.)
- Required disclosures: none found at state level.
- Retention: 2 years. HAR §11-17-14(b): required information must be permanently recorded in ink or indelible pencil in a bound book kept solely for this purpose, available at reasonable hours for examination by the director, kept in the shop for two years from date of last entry. §11-17-14(c): written consents for persons under 18 kept on file for two years.
- ID: none found.
- Artist/license info: HAR §11-17-14(a) — record book must contain (2) customer's name, address, age; (3) branch of service, rate/rank, serial number if customer in armed forces; (4) design and location of tattoo; (5) name of the tattoo artist; (6) customer's signature.
- Barber/cosmetology: none found (2022 Act 73 / SB3077 consolidated barbering and cosmetology licensing; no chemical-service consent provisions located).
- Citations: HRS §321-379; HAR §§11-17-7, 11-17-14.
