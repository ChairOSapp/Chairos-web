# ChairOS Consent-Form Builder — State Law Research Reference
**Legal information for a form template, NOT legal advice.** Researched 2026-10-02 against state legislature sites, health-department/board regulations, and regulation PDFs. Anything that could not be verified in a primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED** — no citations were invented. Statutes and regulations change; verify against counsel before shipping production form copy.

Coverage: all 50 states + DC, tattoo vertical fully researched (minimum age, parental-consent mechanics, state-mandated written disclosures, retention, ID verification, artist/license info on the form), with a light barber/cosmetology pass (chemical-service consent elements, patch tests, straight-razor rules). # PART 1 — UNIVERSAL BASELINE

Elements that appear in nearly every state's requirements or are standard practice. The builder should include these by default everywhere, with state-specific overrides layered on top.

## A. Universal tattoo consent-form elements
1. **Client identity** — full legal name, date of birth, address (many states also require phone).
2. **Photo-ID verification** — government-issued photo ID presented at the time of the procedure; a large majority of states require the ID *type* to be recorded and about half require a *photocopy* retained with the record. Minimum legal age is 18 in the overwhelming majority of states.
3. **Procedure specifics** — description/design of the tattoo, body location, date(s) of procedure, ink colors + manufacturer/catalog number when available (TX, VA, OH, NM explicitly; many counties).
4. **Artist identity** — artist name on the record (near-universal); several states also require the artist's **license number** (OR, MN, MO, MS, AR, OK — record, not necessarily the consent form).
5. **Health screening / disclosure of conditions** — allergies (pigments, latex), diabetes, bleeding disorders/anticoagulants, skin conditions, pregnancy, immune conditions. About 20 states mandate a written health questionnaire or require the client to be asked about specific conditions.
6. **Written acknowledgment of risks** — infection, allergic reaction, permanence, scarring. A dozen+ states require this in writing on the form, several with **verbatim** mandated language (MN, MO, KS, WV, IL-model, MI-model).
7. **Permanence statement** — "should be considered permanent" / removal requires surgery and may scar. Mandated verbatim in MN, MO; conceptually mandated in many others (NJ, NV, MT, VA, WA-rules, IL, AR, MI, CO).
8. **Aftercare instructions, written AND verbal** — the single most universal mandate: ~40 states require written aftercare instructions to be given to the client, typically including signs of infection and direction to consult a physician.
9. **Client signature + artist signature**, with the date. Many states require the signature to attest receipt/understanding of risks and aftercare.
10. **No intoxicated clients** — nearly universal prohibition (tattooing someone visibly under the influence of drugs/alcohol).
11. **Retention** — 2–3 years is the modal requirement (2 yrs is the most common: FL, TX, VA, TN, KY, ME, VT, NC-unverified, HI, IN, WA, NV; 3 yrs: CA-county, MA, NJ, DE, MD, DC, OR, CO, NM, IL-unverified, IA, MN, MI, OK, LA, WI-unverified; outliers: NH 7 yrs, RI 5 yrs, SC 6 yrs, NE 5 yrs for minors, AL "permanent", WV 5 yrs).
12. **Parental-consent tier (where allowed)** — the common mechanics ladder: (a) written consent signed in the artist's presence; (b) parent physically present during the procedure; (c) government-issued photo ID for both minor and parent (copy retained); (d) proof of parentage/guardianship (birth certificate, court order); (e) notarization (FL, DE, KS, KY). About half of states allow parental consent for minors (most with no floor, or 14/16+); about half ban minors outright.

## B. Photo-release
No state mandates a photo release; it is an **optional** add-on everywhere. Treat as opt-in, never pre-checked, with explicit scope (shop portfolio, social media) and revocation language.

## C. Universal barber/salon elements (standard practice, not statute-mandated)
Across all 51 jurisdictions, **no state was found to mandate written consent-form content for ordinary barber/cosmetology services** (cuts, color, relaxers, perms) in statute or board rule. The only verified exceptions found:
- **Iowa** — 481 IAC 940.7(157): signed client consent form **required before** any service using a certified laser product, IPL device, chemical peel, or microdermabrasion; must describe the procedure, list known risks if reasonably determinable, state that disclosures were made and questions answered, and be signed by the client (or person with legal authority).
- **Oregon (cosmetology)** — chemical peels and dermaplaning require documented records: body location, medical/skin conditions, sensitivities, complications, referrals, chemical used (brand/manufacturer, type, layers, time on skin, pre/post care); initial-visit signature that the client received, understood, and consented.
- **Colorado** — 4 CCR 731-1, Rule 1.9(D): predisposition **patch test 24 hours prior** to a chemical/manual resurfacing exfoliating procedure in defined risk cases (physician-supervised skin disorders, pregnancy, recent peel/laser/IPL/surgery); adverse reaction prohibits the product; written physician approval is the alternative.
- **Arkansas (body-art rules, permanent cosmetics)** — patch test on client request or before any permanent cosmetic procedure.
- **Utah** — R156-11a: before chemical exfoliants, microneedling, or microdermabrasion, licensee must inform the client the procedure is cosmetic-only unless under licensed health-care supervision, and must state benefits and risks (written format not specified).

Standard practice the builder should offer as defaults (not state-mandated): identity/DOB, health disclosures and allergies (PPD/henna black-dye allergy for barbers; chemical sensitivity for color), prior chemical-service history, pregnancy disclosure, assumption of risk for chemical services, patch-test acknowledgment/waiver, aftercare instructions, parent/guardian signature for minors receiving chemical services (state rules for minor chemical services were not found in any state — shops set their own policies), straight-razor acknowledgment (no state mandates a straight-razor consent; razors are within normal barber scope, e.g., ARM 24.121 in MT).

## D. How to read this document
- Per-state sections state the **minimum age**, whether **parental consent is allowed** and its mechanics, **every disclosure the state mandates in writing on the consent form**, **retention**, **ID rules**, and **artist/license info required on the form**, with statute/regulation citations.
- "Ban (outright)" = parental consent cannot override. "Consent route" = a minor may be tattooed with parental consent.
- Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be confirmed in a primary source — do not ship form copy based on them.
- County/local regulation matters in: NV (entirely county-regulated), PA, CT, MA (state silent; counties/towns control), CO (age rules county-level), UT (disclosures county-level), NY (consent records county-level), WY (no statewide body-art rules), ID (no state licensing).

# PART 2 — PER-STATE SECTIONS

## FLORIDA *(priority — fully verified)*
- **Min age:** 16. Under 16 banned except medical/dental procedures by ch. 458/459/466 licensees. — Fla. Stat. § 381.00787(1)
- **Parental consent:** allowed for 16–17 only, and **ALL** conditions must be met: (a) minor accompanied by parent/legal guardian; (b) minor AND parent each produce **government-issued photo ID**; (c) parent's **written notarized consent on the department-prescribed form (DH 4146, 7/12)**; (d) parent submits **proof of parent/guardian status**; (e) procedure performed by a licensed tattoo/guest artist or medical/dental licensee. — § 381.00787(2)
- **Penalty:** violation = 2nd-degree misdemeanor. Safe harbor: no violation if the artist carefully inspected an apparently-valid government photo ID showing 18+, the minor misrepresented themselves, and a reasonable person would have believed it genuine/issued to them. — § 381.00787(3)
- **Customer record** (artist completes per customer): artist name; customer name, age, birthdate; tattoo description + body location; customer signature; artist signature; date(s) of procedure. — Fla. Admin. Code 64E-28.009(1)
- **16–17 additions:** inspect copies of gov-issued photo ID for minor AND parent (if minor's ID lacks birthdate, copy of birth certificate); signed notarized DH 4146 consent; full customer record. — 64E-28.009(3)
- **ID defined:** document issued by a state, federal, or foreign government with the person's photo. — 64E-28.002(10)
- **Aftercare:** must be provided to the customer **verbally AND in writing**, before or after the procedure. — 64E-28.009(4)
- **Retention:** customer records (incl. parental consent) kept **2 years**; current licensing period's records kept onsite and available for inspection. Personnel records (artist name, address, DOB, license number) kept 2 years after employment ends. — 64E-28.007(21)(a)–(e)
- **Artist/license info:** active artist license must be displayed publicly at all times; customer record includes artist name. — § 381.00779(2)(a); 64E-28.009(1)(a). Establishment must keep each notarized parental consent for the department-prescribed period (see 2-yr rule). — § 381.00779(3)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** no statute-mandated risk-language on the consent form itself (risks live in the board's aftercare education materials); the mandated written items are the DH 4146 notarized consent, the full customer record, and written aftercare.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found; patch tests not state-mandated; straight razors within normal barber scope with no special consent rule.

## TEXAS *(priority — fully verified)*
- **Min age:** 18. **Exception only:** cover-up of an existing tattoo containing obscene/offensive, gang-related, or drug-related markings (or other markings a parent/guardian/court considers in the minor's best interest to cover), with parental consent. — Tex. Health & Safety Code § 146.012; 25 TAC § 229.406(c)–(d)
- **Parental consent (cover-up only):** parent/guardian **physically present** at time of tattooing; executes **affidavit of parent/guardian status**; presents **evidence of the minor's identity**; presents **evidence of own parent/guardian status**; supplies the studio a **written detailed description or photograph of the tattoo to be covered** (kept with permanent records); parent's full name, address, telephone. — 25 TAC § 229.406(d)(2)
- **ID:** at time of tattooing, client presents valid **government-issued photo ID with photograph AND printed date of birth** (driver's license, passport, military ID). Artist **verifies and documents** age, DOB, and ID type in the permanent client record. — 25 TAC § 229.406(a)–(b)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing** (before tattoo, **verbal AND written**, to client and parent if applicable): at least — (1) possibility of discomfort/pain; (2) **permanence of markings**; (3) risk of infection; (4) possibility of **allergic reaction to pigments/materials**. — 25 TAC § 229.406(h)
- **Retention:** permanent records kept at the studio **≥2 years from the date of last entry**, available to an authorized agent. Contents: client name, address, phone; date; client age, DOB, ID type; **specific ink color(s) + manufacturer/catalog number when available**; body location; artist name; **statement that the client received a copy of the written care instructions and has read and understands them**; client signature. — 25 TAC § 229.406(j); statutory hook § 146.013 (permanent record of each person tattooed for the board-established period; available for inspection)
- **Aftercare:** client (and parent if applicable) must receive **oral AND written** aftercare each session — sun avoidance for 2 weeks, no swimming, cleanse, antibiotic ointment, sterile bandage, consult a practitioner at the first sign of infection/allergy, report to the artist + DSHS at (512) 834-6711. DSHS sample forms are examples, not mandatory; the rule requires the (h)/(j)(7) disclosures + the aftercare-receipt statement. — 25 TAC § 229.406(h), (j)(7); DSHS sample aftercare PDF (rev. 6/23/22)
- **Other:** no tattooing anyone appearing under the influence of alcohol/drugs — 25 TAC § 229.406(f); no tattooing skin with unhealthy conditions (rashes, boils, infections, abrasions) — 25 TAC § 229.406(g). A minor falsely stating age to get a tattoo commits a Class B misdemeanor. — 25 TAC § 229.406(l)
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

## CALIFORNIA *(priority — fully verified)*
- **Min age:** 18, **no exceptions for parental consent**. Tattooing anyone under 18 = misdemeanor. — Cal. Penal Code § 653 (exception only: licensed healing-arts practitioner acting in practice). Cal. H&S Code § 119302(a): client must be 18 to be offered/receive a tattoo or permanent cosmetics, **regardless of parental consent**.
- **Disclosures mandated in writing** — the informed-consent form must be read, completed, and signed by the client **before** the procedure and must include at minimum: (1) description of the procedure; (2) what to expect, suggested care, and medical complications that may occur; (3) **statement of permanence**; (4) **notice that tattoo inks/dyes/pigments have NOT been approved by the FDA and the health consequences are unknown**; (5) postprocedure instructions — site care info, activity restrictions (bathing, recreational water, gardening, contact with animals) + durations, infection signs (redness, swelling, tenderness, red streaks toward the heart, fever, purulent drainage), when to seek medical care. — H&S Code § 119303(a)
- **Mandated health questionnaire** — client completes and signs before the procedure: possible pregnancy; history of herpes at the procedure site, diabetes, latex/antibiotic allergies, hemophilia or other bleeding disorder, cardiac valve disease; medication use including prophylactic antibiotics before dental/surgical procedures; other bloodborne-pathogen risk factors. **§ 119303(b)**
- Medical info handled per HIPAA/state law; no sale/share/transfer of questionnaire data. — § 119303(c)
- **Retention:** the state act sets no duration for general client records — **state-level retention UNVERIFIED**; county programs impose it (e.g., CA county temp-body-art guides: service records — name, address, date, ink colors + manufacturer, placement — kept **2 years**).
- **Artist/license info:** practitioner registration certificate must be displayed prominently at the facility; client records name the practitioner (per county guides: procedure log includes practitioner and client names + date). — H&S § 119312(g). Registration requires: 18+, hep B vaccination/immunity or OSHA declination, bloodborne-pathogen training, self-certification. — H&S § 119306(b)–(d)
- **ID:** no state-prescribed ID-verification procedure; age verification is the shop's duty (counties inspect "customer 18 or older").
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

## NEW YORK *(priority — verified)*
- **Min age:** 18, **no exceptions**. Tattooing a child under 18 = unlawfully dealing with a child in the 2nd degree, a **class B misdemeanor** — regardless of parental consent. — N.Y. Penal Law § 260.21(2) (NY DOH confirms).
- **State-level consent-form statute for tattooing:** none — counties license shops. **NYC example** (county-level rules, representative): **NYC Health Code Ch. 22 § 22-11** — signed written consent per customer listing name, age, DOB, address, telephone, date tattooed, body site; consents retained **2 years** after the tattoo date, available for inspection. **§ 22-12** — written aftercare instructions to prevent infection (sample instructions in DOH publication). **§ 22-13** — complaint sign (NYC DOH, 125 Worth Street) posted where tattooing is performed. NYC DOH tattoo manual advises informing clients of possible risks, asking about ink/latex allergies, advising pregnant women to consult a physician.
- **Piercing (not tattoo):** PHL Art. 4-A requires written parental consent on DOH form DOH 5072, signed in the presence of the shop owner/artist for piercing minors; ear piercing exempt.
- **Retention outside NYC:** county-level, varies — **UNVERIFIED** at state level.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

## GEORGIA *(priority — verified)*
- **Min age:** 18, **no exceptions for regular tattoo shops**. — O.C.G.A. § 16-5-71. Exception: licensed physician/osteopath or technician under direct supervision for medical/cosmetic purposes. DPH Body Art Rules (6)(e): "In accordance with O.C.G.A. Section 16-5-71, no person under the age of eighteen shall be tattooed." Parental consent does **not** override. (O.C.G.A. § 16-12-5: no tattoo within one inch of the eye socket — misdemeanor.)
- **Client records:** for each client, records of identification, an application, client evaluation, and an informed consent form kept in digital or print form, retained minimum **3 years**; kept on premises minimum 1 year; all 3 years available to the Dept./Health Authority on request; stored to prohibit unauthorized access. — GA DPH Body Art Rules (8), authority O.C.G.A. § 31-40-5
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** client must receive and understand **written AND verbal aftercare**. — GA DPH Body Art Rules (7)(d). County rules (e.g., North GA Health District) add, consistent with state rules: pre-procedure client record prepared before the procedure (name + signature, address, proof of age, date, design, location, artist name); statement of informed consent on file; statement by client attesting not under the influence of alcohol/drugs on file; signed copy of aftercare procedures on file; signed copy of risk/complication instructions on file.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

## NORTH CAROLINA *(priority — verified)*
- **Min age:** 18, **no exceptions**. "It shall be unlawful for any person or persons to tattoo the arm, limb, or any part of the body of any other person under 18 years of age." Parental consent does **not** override (unlike piercing, which requires prior consent of the custodial parent). Violation = Class 2 misdemeanor. — N.C.G.S. § 14-400(a)
- **Permits:** artist permits per G.S. 130A-283 + 15A NCAC 18A .3200 (Section .3200 – Tattooing); permit per artist per location, applied for 30 days ahead via the county health department. — 15A NCAC 18A .3202
- **Consent-form contents / retention at state level:** **UNVERIFIED** — the .3200 rules are sanitation/permit-focused; no state-prescribed consent-form elements or retention period found. County health departments may add requirements.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

## ARIZONA *(priority — verified)*
- **Min age:** 18 default; minors may be tattooed **only with the physical presence of a parent/legal guardian**. — A.R.S. § 13-3721(A)(1). Ear-piercing exception: written or verbal permission suffices; health-care-provider-prescribed procedures excepted. — § 13-3721(B)
- **ID:** requesting age ID and relying in good faith on its accuracy = defense to prosecution. — § 13-3721(C)
- **Penalty:** violation = **class 6 felony**. — § 13-3721(D)
- **No state artist licensure, no state-prescribed consent-form contents, no state retention period** — counties/towns regulate shops individually. — AZ SB 1232 fact sheet (2007)
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated written-consent elements found.

### Northeast block

## MAINE
- **Min age:** 18 — outright ban, even with a parent. 32 M.R.S. § 4203: "No person shall place a tattoo mark or figure upon a person under the age of 18 years." Violation: civil, $500–$1,000 per violation. — 32 M.R.S. § 4204
- **Parental consent:** not allowed for tattoos. (Piercing only: prior written parent/guardian consent, parent's ID noted on the record — 32 M.R.S. § 4323; 10-144 C.M.R. ch. 209 § 4(B).)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** medical history obtained; clients with diabetes or severe skin infections advised of added complications — "All clients shall be advised of possible complications from being tattooed"; every client questioned about latex allergy; written aftercare given **out loud and in writing**, signed copy kept — "Permanent records for each patron, including a signed copy of the written aftercare instructions provided to the patron." No tattooing anyone obviously intoxicated. — 10-144 C.M.R. ch. 210 §§ 4(A), 4(W), 6(B)
- **Retention:** patron records (date; name and address; age; picture ID used + serial number; patron's own signature; signed copy of written aftercare) kept at the shop, available on request, minimum **2 years**. The patron personally writes down the picture ID and serial number; the practitioner verifies it. On sale/closing, records go to the Department. — 10-144 C.M.R. ch. 210 § 6(B), (C)
- **ID:** required — picture ID used as proof, with serial number recorded; acceptable: driver's license, liquor ID card, military ID, or other adequate record.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated consent-form elements verified (32 M.R.S. ch. 126; consent provisions UNVERIFIED).

## NEW HAMPSHIRE
- **Min age:** 18 — outright ban. RSA 314-A:8, I prohibits tattooing and branding anyone under 18 (no parental route). (Piercing only: parent physically present + evidence of parent/guardian status + signed declaration naming the piercing location — RSA 314-A:8, III.)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** "Each licensee shall disclose to each client the health risks and potential adverse effects of the procedure prior to beginning any procedure" (Plc 606.04(a) — statute/rule does not specify in writing); **written AND oral aftercare** instructions to every client (Plc 606.04(b)); for minor piercing, risks disclosed to the parent/guardian (RSA 314-A:8, III). No written consent form required for adult tattoos; no health-history form required.
- **Retention:** all records minimum **7 years** (longest in the nation). — RSA 314-A:6, I; Plc 606.03(c)(2). Minor piercing paperwork: 7 years or 1 year past the minor's 18th birthday, whichever is longer. — Plc 606.03(e)
- **ID:** licensees must keep "a photocopy, photograph, or scan of the identification provided" — valid government-issued ID. — Plc 606.03(c)(2). A client who refuses must sign an acknowledgment that ID was checked. — Plc 606.03(d)
- **Note:** towns may layer local ordinances (Salem, Laconia, Hudson do).
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated consent-form elements verified (barber/cosmetology provisions UNVERIFIED).

## VERMONT
- **Min age:** 18 to consent alone; **no floor age** — a minor of any age may be tattooed with proper parental consent.
- **Parental consent:** allowed. **Written** consent of parent/guardian **signed in the tattooist's presence**; must contain the parent's certification that the information is true and accurate "under pains and penalties of perjury"; before the parent signs, the tattooist must disclose to the parent the information required by the rules. Minor, tattooist, and parent each sign (OPR Minor Tattooing Consent Form). — Administrative Rules for Tattooing and Body Piercing § 3.6; 26 V.S.A. § 4102. Violation = unprofessional conduct. — 26 V.S.A. § 4108 (as amended by 2024 Act 158). (Piercing: no state age rule at all — piercers use sound judgment.)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing** — the consent form must include: (1) an explanation of the procedure; (2) that tattooing is **permanent**; (3) that removal may not be complete and may leave **scarring**; (4) complications — bruising, scarring, bleeding, skin or blood infection, allergic reactions to inks or jewelry, nerve and tissue damage (plus oral-piercing risks); (5) **mandated questions**: alcohol or drugs in last 8 hours; whether client ate within last 2 hours; heart valve conditions (mitral valve prolapse), herpes, latex and antibiotic allergies, diabetes, hemophilia/bleeding disorders, blood thinners (Coumadin, Warfarin, aspirin), autoimmune disorders, pregnancy; (6) client signature and date. — Code Vt. R. 04-030-310 § 3.2(F)(1)–(2). Written AND verbal aftercare required covering wound care, what healing looks like, activity limits, signs of infection. — § 3.3(E). § 4.1 disclosures made by posted notice, with a separate document acknowledging them.
- **Retention:** client file (name, address, phone, procedure date, medical conditions disclosed, procedure description, **practitioner's name**) kept minimum **2 years** from the date of first treatment, available to the Director on inspection. — § 3.2(E)(9). Electronic files allowed with client signature (OPR FAQs). On sale/closing, records go to the Director.
- **ID:** no state-mandated client photo-ID rule found for tattoos (**UNVERIFIED**). Parent identity established via the in-person signed certification under penalties of perjury.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated consent-form elements verified (26 V.S.A. ch. 29; details UNVERIFIED).

## MASSACHUSETTS
- **Min age:** 18 — no minors, even with a parent (per the DPH Model Regulations for Body Art Establishments § 4(A), as locally adopted — e.g., Haverhill; Boston Body Art Regulation § 5.8.1). "No tattooing, piercing of genitalia, branding or scarification shall be performed on a person under the age of 18." — Model § 4(A)
- **Parental consent:** not allowed for tattoos. (Piercing, non-genital: parent/legal custodial parent/legal guardian present, properly identified, signs a consent form — Model § 4(A)–(B); genital piercing banned under 18 regardless.)
- **No statewide statute; local boards of health regulate under MGL ch. 111, § 31.** State-level form requirements: none — local rules control.
- **Disclosures mandated in writing (model):** (a) artist informs client **verbally and in writing** that these conditions may increase risks: diabetes, hemophilia, skin disease, pigment allergies, epilepsy, blood thinners, hepatitis, HIV, latex sensitivity; (b) client signs a form confirming the information was provided, that they have no disqualifying condition, that they consent, and that they received written aftercare — Model §§ 6(D)(1)–(2); (c) **verbal and written aftercare** covering cleaning, when to call a doctor, and the shop's address and phone — Model § 6(K).
- **Retention:** client records (name, DOB, address, procedure date, **practitioner name**, procedure description/body location, signed consent; under-18: parent ID, presence, consent) kept in a secure place minimum **3 years**, available to the Board on request. — Model § 5(E)
- **ID:** Model requires a copy of the **parent's photo ID** for under-18 clients; Boston requires the client's own valid proof of identification on the consent form. No state-level rule; varies by board.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated patch test, chemical-service disclosure, or minor-consent element verified under 240 CMR.

## RHODE ISLAND
- **Min age:** 18 — outright ban, except medical purposes only. "Every person who shall tattoo any minor under the age of eighteen (18)" = misdemeanor, up to 1 yr and/or $300. — R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-9-15(a)
- **Parental consent:** only via the narrow medical exception — **ALL** required: parent/guardian present; **government-issued photo ID for BOTH** minor and parent; **written notarized parental consent** on the department's form; proof of parentage/guardianship; **notarized physician** (licensed under R.I. Gen. Laws ch. 5-37) consent; work by a licensed artist. — R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-9-15(b). (Piercing: parent/guardian present + written consent + parent's proper photo ID — § 23-1-39(c); 216-RICR-40-10-14 §§ 14.4.5(E)(1), 14.5.8(A)(4).)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** no written consent or health-history questions mandated by rule (artist must visually examine the area for sores, lesions, wounds, skin disease, or signs of drug use). Aftercare mandatory **verbally and in writing**, instructing the patron to consult a physician at the first sign of infection. — 216-RICR-40-10-15 § 15.4.5(A)(3)(n). Patrons of an **apprentice** must be informed **in writing** and acknowledge via dated signature. — § 15.4.3(B)(4)
- **Retention:** patron record (date tattooed; name, address, phone, age; copy of photo ID; tattoo location/design; **artist's name**) entered in a **bound book with pre-numbered pages**, kept minimum **5 years** after the date tattooed. — 216-RICR-40-10-15 § 15.5.7
- **ID:** required — "Photo identification as proof of age (e.g., driver's license, military identification), a copy of which shall be maintained for each patron." — § 15.5.7(A)(2)(a). Affirmative defense to § 11-9-15: inspected plausible government photo ID showing 18+, photocopy attached to the information card. — § 11-9-15(c)
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated consent-form elements verified (216-RICR-40-05-4 covers licensing; consent provisions UNVERIFIED).

## CONNECTICUT
- **Min age:** 18 to consent alone; **no floor age** — unemancipated minor under 18 needs parent/guardian permission.
- **Parental consent:** allowed. The statute does **not** require the permission to be written, notarized, in-person, or the parent present. — Conn. Gen. Stat. § 20-266p(6). (Piercing: **written** permission of the minor's PARENT required — guardian not named; presence not required — § 19a-92g(a).) Tattooing an unemancipated minor under 18 without parent/guardian permission = class D misdemeanor. — § 20-266p(6)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** **none at state level** — no disclosure, health-history, or aftercare mandates found (town ordinances may impose them).
- **Retention:** **no state retention period and no state list of client-record contents** — set locally by town/district health departments.
- **ID:** **no state ID rule.** Photo-ID check is the practical way to establish 18, since the age rule turns on it.
- **Barber/salon:** no state-mandated consent-form elements verified (CT DPH technician licensing §§ 20-266o et seq.; barber/cosmetology provisions UNVERIFIED).

## NEW JERSEY
- **Min age:** 18 to consent alone; **no floor age** — under 18 allowed with written parental consent.
- **Parental consent:** allowed. **Written** consent of parent or legal guardian required; **parent/guardian must accompany the client** during the procedure; **government-issued photo ID of the client AND the parent** photocopied and kept with the application. — N.J.A.C. 8:27-4.2(c). Tattooing a minor without written parental permission = disorderly persons offense. — N.J.S.A. 2C:40-21. (Emancipated-minor carve-out in the rule conflicts with the statute — rely on the statute.)

- **Retention:** all client records (name, DOB, proof of age, address; emergency contact; procedure date; **artist name**; procedure type/location; client signature; minors: proof of parental presence + written consent) minimum **3 years**, available to the health official on request. — N.J.A.C. 8:27-4.2(a), (b)1
- **ID:** required — "Government issued photographic identification of the client shall be photocopied and maintained on file with the client's application" and "Identification of the parent or legal guardian shall be photocopied." — N.J.A.C. 8:27-4.2(c)
- **Barber/salon:** no client consent elements found — NJ Board of Cosmetology and Hairstyling (N.J.A.C. 13:28) verified only for shop/practitioner records (shop keeps practitioner records 2 years — N.J.A.C. 13:28-3.3(d)); no patch-test or chemical-service consent found.

## DELAWARE
- **Min age:** 18 to consent alone; **no floor age** — minor (under 18, unemancipated) may be tattooed with proper parental consent.

- **ID:** required — copy of the client's state or federally issued photo ID with birthdate kept in the record (driver's license, state ID, passport, immigration card) — § 6.3; statute attaches a photocopy to the information card the client completes — 11 Del. C. § 1114(d)(2).

## MARYLAND
- **Min age:** **none statewide** — no statewide minimum age; written consent governs. ## DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
- **Min age:** 18 to consent alone; **16–17** with written parental consent; no tattoo at all under 16.
- **Parental consent:** allowed, in two tiers. "No body artist shall perform body art procedures on a person under 18 years of age; except, that, if a parent or legal guardian has provided their written consent" and "Tattooing services are permitted for persons 16 years of age and older." — D.C. Code § 47-2853.76e(b). Parent presence **not** required by statute. Ear piercing at any age with written parental consent; all other piercings banned under 18, no consent route. **CAUTION:** DC Health's 2017 rules (25-G DCMR § 708.2) still say 18 for all body art; the 2024 statute (D.C. ## VIRGINIA
- **Min age:** 18. Va. Code §18.2-371.3 — no tattoo on anyone under 18 **except** (i) in the presence of the parent/guardian, or (ii) by/under supervision of licensed medical personnel.
- **Parental consent:** allowed — only as the §18.2-371.3 exception (parent/guardian **present**). 18VAC41-50-410(E): the client **and** parent/guardian "if applicable" receive disclosures verbally and in writing on the board-prescribed form, with both signatures required.
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** **18VAC41-50-410(E)** — before tattooing, each client (and parent/guardian if applicable) must be informed **verbally AND in writing, using the client disclosure form prescribed by the board** (Board Form **A450-12TDIS**), with signatures of both client and tattooer acknowledging receipt. Board-prescribed form content (quoted): the procedure is **invasive and penetrates the skin**; **risk of infection including bloodborne pathogens (HIV, hepatitis B/C)**; **allergic reactions to dyes, pigments, or metals**; **pain, and the artist cannot legally provide anesthesia**; risks list of **pain, infection, allergic reaction, permanence of the tattoo, and risks of removal**.

- **Min age:** 18. — Mo. Rev. Stat. § 324.520(2)
- **Parental consent:** allowed at any age under 18. § 324.520(2): no tattooing/branding/piercing a minor without **prior written informed consent** of the parent/legal guardian; the parent must execute the consent **in the presence of the person performing the procedure or an employee/agent of the establishment**. Fraudulent misrepresentation as a parent = class B misdemeanor.
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** 20 CSR 2267-5.010 §(3)(A)(2) — before tattooing, the practitioner must inform each patron **in person and in writing** of: (A) the dangers for persons with (1) impaired kidney/liver function, (2) diabetes, (3) jaundice within the 12 months prior, (4) blood-thinner therapy, (5) immunocompromising medication; and (B) **verbatim**: "that the tattoo body piercing and/or branding should be considered permanent, and can only be removed or repaired with a surgical procedure that may leave permanent scarring and disfigurement." The form states the patron was informed in person and in writing and agrees to the procedure, is retained at the establishment, and bears the **practitioner's signature attesting review, in-person + written advisement, and date**. Licensed tattooists must also retain **dye records including the lot number of each pigment for each patron** — § 5.010(1)(B). 20 CSR 2267-5.040(2): **verbal and written** aftercare instructions after every procedure.
- **Retention:** **minimum 2 years** after completion (multi-visit work: 2 years following completion). Patron record: (A) name, address, telephone, DOB; (B) **driver's license number or official picture ID number** (under-18: parent's DL/picture ID number); (C) signature (parent for under-18); (D) medical/health form covering controlled-substance meds, HIV/Hep B/other bloodborne diseases, allergies, healing-affecting diseases including diabetes, illegal substances, alcohol use, jaundice within 12 months, blood thinners, immunocompromising meds; (E) signed consent form; plus **name, license number, and signature of practitioner** and note of any adverse effects. — 20 CSR 2267-5.020 §§ (1), (7). Establishment must post a conspicuous sign stating no under-18 tattoo/pierce/brand and keep license + 2"×2" photo of each practitioner conspicuously displayed. — 2267-5.020 §(4); 2267-3.010(F)
- **ID:** the establishment must **verify through proper picture ID that the patron is at least 18** — 2267-5.020 §(5); record the patron's DL number or official picture ID number (parent's ID number when the patron is under 18).
- **Barber/salon:** none found in codified law. (A "parental consent" definition for genital-area body waxing on minors appears in proposed Missouri cosmetology bills — e.g., SB 1374 (2012), HB 1434 (2024), HB 52 (2025) — but not in codified RSMo § 329.010 per the 2016 statute text; treat as proposed, not law.)
- **Citations:** Mo. Rev. Stat. § 324.520(2); 20 CSR 2267-5.010, -5.020, -5.040, -3.010 (sos.mo.gov, current through 6/30/24).

## WISCONSIN

## ILLINOIS
- **Min age:** 18 — **outright ban**. 720 ILCS 5/12C-35 "Tattooing the body of a minor": anyone not a licensed doctor may not tattoo or offer to tattoo a person under 18, knowingly or recklessly; an owner/employee may not permit an under-18 to enter or remain where tattooing is performed unless accompanied by a parent/legal guardian. **Class A misdemeanor.** Narrow exceptions: an under-18 tattooing another under-18 away from business premises; tattoo removal for trafficking victims/former gang members at a registered establishment. (Piercing only: 720 ILCS 5/12C-40 — no piercing under 18 without **written consent** of parent/legal guardian; oral-cavity consent must include the quoted warning: *"I understand that the oral piercing of the tongue, lips, cheeks, or any other area of the oral cavity carries serious risk of infection or damage to the mouth and teeth, or both infection and damage to those areas, that could result but is not limited to nerve damage, numbness, and life threatening blood clots."*)
- **Disclosures mandated in writing:** the IDPH educational form (IOCI 0177-10, "What to Know Before Getting a Tattoo," required educational materials per 77 Ill. Adm. Code 797.600(a)(c)) requires discussion of: tattoo **permanence** ("may only take a few minutes to acquire, it is permanent"); **bloodborne-disease** risks (hepatitis C, hepatitis B, tetanus, HIV/AIDS via contaminated equipment); **skin infections** (minor to serious, including antibiotic-resistant; redness, swelling, pus-like drainage); **granulomas**; **scars and keloids**; **allergic reactions** (itchy rash); **swelling or burning during MRI**; plus BBP training, proficiency, spore-test results; instructions to **contact a physician immediately** on abnormal itching, irritation, redness, swelling, or fever. Health screening (yes/no) must cover: diabetes; hemophilia; skin diseases/lesions/sensitivities; allergies/adverse reactions to pigments, dyes, or latex; epilepsy/seizures/fainting/narcolepsy; anticoagulants; HIV; hepatitis; other relevant info. Form fields include: **establishment name/address/phone; client name + signature; artist name + artist signature; date; and a copy of the client's photo ID if collected.** 77 Ill. Adm. Code 797.600(b) also requires aftercare instructions.
- **Retention:** **UNVERIFIED** — the verbatim 77 Ill. Adm. Code 797 text was not retrievable from primary online sources.

- **Barber/salon:** none found at state level (68 Ill. Adm. Code 1175.305 covers sanitation only; no client-consent/patch-test rule surfaced).
- **Citations:** 720 ILCS 5/12C-35; 720 ILCS 5/12C-40; 410 ILCS 54 §§ 15, 25, 35, 80, 85; IDPH form IOCI 0177-10 "What to Know Before Getting a Tattoo" (per 77 Ill. Adm. Code 797.600).

## MICHIGAN

- **ID:** for minors — parent/guardian photo ID as above; record documents client name/address/age-DOB. No state-level license number for individual artists on the form (individual artists are NOT licensed by the state — MDHHS FAQ #5; facility licensing only, PA 375 (2010); some localities license artists).
